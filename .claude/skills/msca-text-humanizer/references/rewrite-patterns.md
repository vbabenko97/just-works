# Rewrite patterns

These examples illustrate the target style. Preserve the user's facts and terminology; the examples are patterns, not phrases to copy blindly.

## 1. Remove generic promotional framing

Before:
> The proposed research project represents a highly innovative and comprehensive endeavour that aims to leverage advanced methodologies in order to significantly advance the current state of the art.

After:
> The project combines [method A] and [method B] to address [specific gap]. Its main advance is [verifiable advance] compared with [current approach].

Why it works: the rewrite replaces adjectives with the actual gap, method and advance.

## 2. Make training sound credible

Before:
> The fellowship will provide me with a unique opportunity to substantially enhance my professional profile through a broad range of cutting-edge training activities.

After:
> The fellowship will address two gaps in my current profile: [competence 1] and [competence 2]. I will develop these skills through [training/activity], with [output or evidence] used to demonstrate progress.

Why it works: career development becomes a mechanism rather than a promise.

## 3. Keep the applicant voice professional but not over-polished

Before:
> My academic journey has endowed me with a robust interdisciplinary foundation and a profound commitment to pursuing impactful research at the interface of multiple scientific domains.

After:
> I completed my PhD at Igor Sikorsky Kyiv Polytechnic Institute in 2025. My doctoral work gave me a strong basis in [field/method], while the proposed fellowship would extend this expertise toward [new area].

Why it works: the language is straightforward, specific and plausible for an early-career researcher. Do not fill the placeholders without evidence from the user.

## 4. Tighten impact without weakening it

Before:
> The anticipated scientific, societal and economic impacts of the project are expected to be significant and far-reaching, contributing to the advancement of knowledge, supporting stakeholders, and potentially generating long-term benefits for society.

After:
> Scientifically, the project will deliver [output] for [research community/use]. Its direct societal contribution is [supported pathway], while the economic relevance is limited to [supported mechanism]. Broader effects depend on later uptake and are therefore not claimed as direct project impacts.

Why it works: it separates impact types and removes unsupported scale.

## 5. Replace repetitive transitions

Before:
> Furthermore, the method will be validated on dataset A. Moreover, the results will be compared with baseline B. Additionally, uncertainty will be quantified using method C.

After:
> The method will be validated on dataset A and compared with baseline B. Uncertainty will be quantified using method C.

Why it works: the logical sequence does not need three signposts.

## 6. Preserve technical terms instead of thesaurus substitutions

Before:
> The predictive architecture will be instructed on the developmental cohort and scrutinized on an autonomous population.

After:
> The predictive model will be trained on the development cohort and evaluated on an independent population.

Why it works: standard technical wording sounds more credible than unusual synonyms.

## 7. Do not manufacture ESL errors

Bad humanization:
> This project give possibility to develop important skills and make strong impact for European science.

Target:
> This project will develop the skills I need in [area] and produce [specific output] for [specific scientific use].

Why it works: a B2-C1 researcher can write correct English. Deliberate mistakes look artificial and can damage reviewer confidence.
