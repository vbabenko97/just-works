# Start-from-zero interview

Ask only the next useful batch. Maintain a fact ledger and do not force the applicant to answer irrelevant questions.

## Stage 1: eligibility and mobility
Ask for:
1. European Fellowship confirmation and intended beneficiary country/host.
2. PhD award date, or unconditional defence date if degree not yet formally awarded.
3. Research-experience periods since PhD relevant to the 8-year FTE rule and any possible exclusions/extensions.
4. Residence and main-activity history for the 36 months before 9 September 2026, plus enough history to populate Part A without gaps.
5. Any PF/EU proposal in the previous call involving the same researcher and recruiting organisation and, if so, its score.
6. Intended fellowship duration, secondment(s), and any non-academic placement.

Do not pronounce eligibility until the relevant official 2026 rule has been checked.

## Stage 2: scientific core
Ask:
- one-sentence problem;
- what current practice/literature cannot do;
- central research question/hypothesis;
- 3-5 objectives;
- data/materials and access status;
- main method;
- primary success/verification measures;
- strongest fallback if a key assumption fails.

## Stage 3: researcher-host fit
Ask:
- applicant's 3-5 most relevant achievements/skills;
- 2-4 genuine competence gaps;
- supervisor and host strengths that fill those gaps;
- expertise the applicant uniquely transfers to the host;
- supervision arrangements and any known continuity risk.

## Stage 4: impact and implementation
Ask:
- desired career state 2-5 years after the fellowship;
- publications/software/data/protocol/grant/network/leadership outcomes;
- scientific users, potential exploiters, public audiences;
- WPs, major dependencies, external approvals/access, compute/resources;
- top scientific and administrative risks;
- ethics/security/data-protection issues.

Then build a proposal architecture before writing dense prose.
