## Part B-1 page pressure

The official 2026 Part B template states that Sections 1, 2 and 3 together must not exceed 10 pages. Tables, figures, references and other elements belonging to those sections count toward the limit. Excess pages can become invisible to evaluators after the deadline.

Therefore, humanization should normally reduce or preserve length rather than expand it. Do not spend scarce space on generic transitions, decorative adjectives, repeated context, or empty claims of excellence.

## Core Part B-1 structure

Preserve the official headings and their evaluator purpose:

1. Excellence
   - 1.1 Research and innovation objectives, ambition and state of the art
   - 1.2 Methodology, interdisciplinarity, relevant gender/diversity dimension, and open science
   - 1.3 Supervision, training, and two-way transfer of knowledge
   - 1.4 Researcher's professional experience, competences and skills
2. Impact
   - 2.1 Career perspectives, employability and skills development
   - 2.2 Dissemination, exploitation and communication measures
   - 2.3 Magnitude and importance of scientific, societal and economic impacts
3. Quality and Efficiency of the Implementation
   - 3.1 Work plan, risks and effort
   - 3.2 Host/participating-organisation capacity and hosting arrangements

Do not move content between headings merely because the prose flows better if that weakens criterion coverage.

For Impact 2.3, prefer credible direct contributions and quantified estimates where they are meaningful and supported. Avoid pretending that a small fellowship directly causes broad societal transformation.

## Generative-AI requirements in the 2026 template

The 2026 application form includes specific guidance on generative AI. Its practical implications for this skill are:

- applicants remain responsible for proposal content, including AI-assisted text;
- AI-generated content and citations should be reviewed and validated for accuracy and appropriateness;
- citations and sources should be checked against originals;
- applicants should consider plagiarism risk;
- AI limitations, including possible bias, errors and knowledge gaps, must be acknowledged;
- applicants must be transparent about which AI tools were used and how they were used.

Never tell the user that humanizing text eliminates these obligations. Do not remove an AI-use disclosure merely to make the proposal appear fully unaided.

## Evaluator-readable style

Use language that helps an evaluator locate the answer to the criterion quickly:
- state the gap before the advance;
- state the objective before the work required to achieve it;
- connect training to a competence gap and a concrete output;
- distinguish what the researcher learns from what the host gains;
- link dissemination/exploitation/communication actions to audience and purpose;
- link impact claims to project outputs and plausible uptake;
- state risks with mitigation rather than pretending there are no risks.

Human-sounding prose is useful only if the evaluator can still score the criterion. A charming paragraph that hides the evidence is just expensive page furniture.
