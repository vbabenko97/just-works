# Default review output

Adapt length to the material supplied. For a full proposal, use the full structure below.

## 1. Verdict

One compact paragraph:
- fundable-looking / borderline / non-competitive in its current form;
- the 2-4 biggest reasons;
- whether any blocker makes scoring secondary.

Do not call this an official decision.

## 2. Estimated evaluator scores

Report:
- Excellence: X.X / 5, weighted contribution XX.X / 50
- Impact: X.X / 5, weighted contribution XX.X / 30
- Implementation: X.X / 5, weighted contribution XX.X / 20
- Weighted total: XX.X / 100

Then state: "Informed simulation, not an official score or funding probability."

Do not infer a universal funding cut-off above the formal 70% threshold unless a current official panel cut-off is available.

## 3. Blockers

List only genuine blocker-level issues. For each:
- what is wrong or unverified;
- why it matters;
- what evidence/action resolves it.

If none, write: "No blocker identified in the material reviewed." Do not imply unreviewed files are safe.

## 4. Top fixes, ranked by likely scoring impact

Rank 5-10 items. For each use this compact pattern:

### 1. [short fix title] - [BLOCKER/MAJOR/MODERATE/MINOR]
**Where:** section/subsection
**Evaluator objection:** one sentence in evaluator language.
**Why this costs points:** specific mechanism.
**Repair:** exact change to make.
**Evidence/confirmation needed:** source, host confirmation, literature check, or "none".
**Page move:** add / replace / compress / cut, with a brief suggestion for what can be removed if space is needed.

Do not order by editing convenience.

## 5. Criterion-by-criterion review

### Excellence
State the score drivers for 1.1-1.4. Separate strengths worth preserving from shortcomings that suppress the score.

### Impact
State the score drivers for 2.1-2.3. Explicitly test career transformation and the result-to-impact causal chain.

### Quality and efficiency of implementation
State the score drivers for 3.1-3.2. Explicitly test work-plan traceability, risk quality, effort plausibility, and host enablement.

## 6. Unsupported or risky claims

Identify:
- novelty/"first" claims needing literature verification;
- quantitative claims needing a source;
- host commitments needing confirmation;
- claims that overreach the study design or project duration.

Give a safer wording direction where useful.

## 7. Cross-section contradictions

Compare, when available:
- Part A vs B-1 vs B-2;
- CV vs researcher narrative;
- objectives vs methods vs WPs;
- Gantt vs deliverables/milestones;
- training vs career plan;
- ethics/security answers vs methods;
- host capacity vs resource claims.

Distinguish contradiction from merely missing information.

## 8. Page-budget advice

If B-1 is near 10 pages, give a short "add / cut" exchange:
- what must be added;
- what can be removed or compressed to fund that space.

Favor evaluator-critical information over background prose.

## 9. Submission-readiness gate

Return GREEN, AMBER, or RED with 1-3 reasons.

GREEN requires current rules/template checked, no unresolved blockers, strong internal consistency, and only minor remaining weaknesses.

AMBER means material score/readiness risks remain.

RED means unsafe to submit because of blocker-level or severe scoring problems.
